## 1. Ordering and gating

Order rounds by cost and by how often they disqualify. Values and core reasoning are where most candidates fall, so test them early and cheaply. Typical funnel:

1. **Resume screen** - recruiting first pass with a keyword guide; manager sees borderline only.
2. **Recruiting pre-screen** - logistics and motivation match.
3. **Culture screen** - values gate, run by the hiring manager.
4. **Core skill screen** - hard-to-teach reasoning and standards.
5. **Final round + debrief** - all priorities observed; decide within 24 hours.

Each step is a gate with a written stop rule, applied the same day.

## 2. Rules for each round

- **Own specific signals.** List which traits each round scores. If two rounds probe the same thing the same way, cut one.
- **Pair said with done.** Mix a stated probe with an observed one in each round.
- **Script the minutes.** Timing table, scripted questions and follow-ups, strong/weak listen-fors, one skip-if-short block, go/no-go line.
- **Realistic preview.** Each interviewer shares one honest hard part of the job (retention).
- **Combine late rounds to save days.** A final session can cover strategy, ownership and collaboration with three interviewers; one leads each block, others off camera.
- **Lace guidance into the round.** Trap rules, recovery ladder, role-play prep and AI-assistance notes go where they are used.
- **Candidate questions every round.** Count them and note depth (inquisitiveness). Once recruiting answers process questions, round Q&A is only about team and work.

## 3. Time budgets and standard slots

- Set the hiring-team budget per candidate first, including 5-min scorecards and the debrief; then fit rounds to it.
- Use standard lengths for calls: 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60. Show both the candidate invite and the interviewer calendar hold (invite + scorecard, and for the final round + debrief).
- Rounding up beats cutting a probe; say when it changes the total, and offer the cleanest cut if the user wants time back.
- Recruiting/HR time is a separate budget (about 10-15 min per candidate is typical).
- When compressing, cut in this order: duplicate stated probes → walk-throughs readable from the resume → written scenarios (replace with verbal) → separate final sessions (merge). Never cut the gates or the only observed probe for a priority trait. Show a table of what was cut, its cost and the mitigation.

## 4. Delegation to HR/recruiting

| Delegate | Keep with the hiring team |
| --- | --- |
| Resume first pass with a keyword guide; borderline to the manager | Culture gate and every values judgment |
| Pre-screen: motivation, logistics, process questions | Anything technical or domain-specific |
| Scheduling; prep emails with ground rules, tool links and any domain primer for outside candidates | Selling the role and team |
| Pre-filled scorecards; chasing them within 2 hours | Scoring, vetoes, the decision |
| Debrief summary sheet: score matrix, flagged 1s, disagreements of 2+, thin values; scan for protected characteristics | Interpreting references |
| Reference calls from a fixed script, in parallel with the final round | |
| Weekly tally of questions drawing "I don't know" | |

Guardrails for recruiting: don't describe team values or what interviewers look for; don't assess technical depth or fit; stop a candidate only on logistics.

**Keyword guide format** (resume first pass): a table with columns Signal / Qualifies / Borderline: send to HM / Does not qualify alone, rows for Technical, Program or ownership, and Plus (ties only). When unsure, send to borderline; a missed candidate costs more than two minutes of manager time in a niche pool.

**Pre-screen script (10 min + 5 min follow-up)**
- 0-1 Welcome and process overview (round names and lengths).
- 1-4 Motivation: "What made you apply?" "What are you hoping to do more of in your next role?" Record verbatim; don't score.
- 4-8 Logistics knockouts: work authorization (plain question only), on-site expectations [X] days at [location], start timing, availability for all rounds within two weeks.
- 8-10 Process questions and next steps; team/technical questions are noted for the HM.
- +5 Log notes, mark background (domain or outside), pre-fill the next scorecard, send prep email, book the next round.

**Prep email template**
```
Subject: Your interview with [team] - format and what to expect
Hi [name],
Your [round] is on [date/time] by video: [link]. Please join with camera on, from a quiet place with a stable connection. If we get disconnected, we'll call you at [phone].
Ground rules for every round: please don't use AI tools or outside help, think out loud, and "I don't know" is always a fine answer.
[Skill/final rounds: we'll use a shared whiteboard at [link]; please open and test it before the call.]
[Outside-domain candidates only: short context note on the domain, without revealing problem specifics.]
Looking forward to it,
[name]
```

**Reference script (finalists, ~15 min):** ask for at least one former peer or partner, not only managers. "Tell me about a time they delivered bad news. When did you hear it?" "When they disagreed with someone senior, what happened?" "Would [partner team] miss them? Why?" "What should their next manager focus on developing?" Record verbatim.

## 5. Speed mechanics

In niche pools the best candidates are off the market in about two weeks. Speed comes from removing waiting, not rigor.

- Standing weekly interviewer holds for each round.
- Same-day go/no-go after each gate; debrief within 24 hours of the final round (or inside the final round's hold).
- References in parallel with the final round; offer approvals agreed before it starts.
- Target first contact to offer in 10-15 business days; no gap over 3 days between steps.
- A short note to the candidate after every round with the next step and date.
- Thin evidence → one short follow-up within 2 days, not a restart.

## 6. Online execution

- Prep email carries ground rules and tool checks; rounds open with a 10-second reminder.
- Camera on; backup phone number; if over 10 minutes lost, reschedule that session, never compress.
- Silence and facial reactions read stronger on video: stay neutral, let pauses run, wait for full answers.
- Make candidates sketch or work live on a shared whiteboard; it resists recited or AI-assisted answers. Vary one parameter of any shared scenario per candidate.
- In multi-interviewer sessions, only the block lead has camera on.
- Don't record; take verbatim notes on paper or a second screen.

## 7. Worked example: 2-hour all-in process

From a real design for a niche advanced controls engineering role (hiring priorities: courage, grit, AI-native; values: transparency, high agency, helping others). Adapt the content, keep the shape.

| Step | Invite | Hold | Owner | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Resume screen | - | HM ~10 min/week (borderline) | HR first pass | Program + one technical path |
| HR pre-screen | 10 | 15 | HR | Logistics only |
| R2 Culture | 20 | 25 | HM | Culture |
| R3 Technical | 30 | 35 | Senior engineer | Reasoning + standards |
| R4 Final + debrief | 45 | 60 | HM, senior engineer, partner (process) engineer | Hire bar |
| Hiring team total | 95 live | 120 | | |

**R2 (20):** 0-6 transparency (timeline pinning) · 6-11 agency + self-optimization (unasked improvement) · 11-13 AI-native (last three uses) · 13-17 courage (cost pinning) · 17-20 candidate questions.

**R3 (30):** 0-15 diagnostic problem with withheld information, fundamentals woven into follow-ups, transfer question, recovery ladder · 15-18 false-premise trap (metric jump = success?) · 18-23 false-premise trap (high R² model fed straight into a live system) · 23-28 live review of AI output with one subtle flaw · 28-30 candidate questions. Outside-domain candidates get a short primer in the prep email.

**R4 (45 + 5 scorecards + 10 debrief):** 0-7 funding choice + pushback + "what would change your mind" (HM) · 7-12 program angle + "when did you almost quit" + share-of-credit bracketing (HM) · 12-17 technical decision drill on the same project, chosen by the interviewer (senior engineer) · 17-27 role-play: partner wants the system turned off, escalates emotionally then by authority (partner engineer, scores courage; private question "would I seek this person out?") · 27-35 pair problem with planted error (senior engineer) · 35-40 "who got better because of you" (skip if short) · 40-45 questions + reveal role-play setup; reveal planted error only if caught · 45-50 scorecards · 50-60 debrief.
